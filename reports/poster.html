When Training Labels Change Who Gets Screened

Temporal evaluation of diagnosis-based versus biomarker-augmented labels in NHANES under finite screening capacity

Cao Thien An Nguyen
Undergraduate, Economics & Data Science
University of Wisconsin–Madison • 2026

01 The Clinical Problem

Screening triage algorithms often train on self-reported prior diagnoses or electronic health record proxies. However, clinical diagnoses require regular clinical encounters, misclassifying underserved individuals facing access barriers as healthy baselines.

02 Study Design & Temporal Protocol

We evaluate whether augmenting self-reported diagnosis with objective laboratory biomonitoring alters triage priority, holding non-invasive predictors and screening capacity constant.

03 Methods & Implementation Note

Primary models are L2-regularized logistic regressions tuned via 5-fold cross-validation on median-imputed, standard-scaled anthropometrics (age, sex, BMI, waist circumference). Poverty Income Ratio (PIR) and insurance coverage were strictly excluded from model inputs and reserved solely for equity audits. Data pipeline ingested 10 XPT files across two survey eras using an embedded DuckDB lakehouse; Pandera contracts validated biological ranges (HbA1c: 2.0% to 25.0%) and confirmed 0 pregnant participants.

Key Takeaway
Changing training labels altered screening priorities and identified two additional potential cases at the same screening capacity.

06 Multi-Architecture Robustness

Re-evaluated triage using alternative decision tree algorithms:

  • Random Forest: Model A detects 60 cases; Model B detects 61 cases (+1 case, 77 swaps).
  • HistGradientBoosting: Model A detects 59 cases; Model B detects 59 cases (0 net gain, 132 swaps).

Confirms priority shifts occur across architectures, while overall yield gains remain modest (+0 to +2 cases).

07 Limitations & Scope

  • Surveillance vs Diagnosis: Single venous HbA1c ≥ 6.5% represents an elevated screening criterion, not an autonomous clinical diagnosis.
  • Inference Bounds: Separate survey-weighted confidence intervals overlap; paired variance estimation is required for confirmatory testing.
  • Operational Scope: Assumes 100% screening adherence among prioritized adults and uniform laboratory costs.
