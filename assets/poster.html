When Training Labels Change Who Gets Screened

Temporal evaluation of diagnosis-based versus biomarker-augmented labels for diabetes screening in NHANES

Cao Thien An Nguyen
Undergraduate, Economics & Data Science
University of Wisconsin–Madison • 2026

01 The Clinical Problem

Population screening triage algorithms frequently train on self-reported prior diagnoses or electronic health record proxies. However, clinical diagnoses require regular clinical encounters, misclassifying unrecorded disease as healthy baselines among underserved individuals facing access barriers.

02 Study Design & Temporal Protocol

We evaluate whether augmenting self-reported diagnosis with objective venous HbA1c (glycated hemoglobin ≥ 6.5%) alters triage priorities in CDC NHANES (National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey), holding predictors and capacity constant.

03 Methods & Implementation Note

Primary models are L2-regularized logistic regressions tuned via 5-fold cross-validation on median-imputed, standard-scaled numeric features (age, BMI, waist circumference) and binary one-hot encoded sex. Poverty Income Ratio (PIR) and insurance were strictly excluded from model inputs and reserved solely for equity audits. Data pipeline ingested 10 XPT files across two survey periods using an embedded DuckDB lakehouse; Pandera contracts validated biological ranges (HbA1c: 2.0%–25.0%) and confirmed 0 pregnant participants.

Key Finding
Changing training labels altered screening priorities and identified two additional potential cases at the same screening capacity.

06 Multi-Architecture Robustness

Triage re-evaluated under alternative tree architectures:

  • Random Forest: Model A detects 60 cases; Model B detects 61 cases (+1 case, 77 swaps).
  • HistGradientBoosting: Model A detects 59 cases; Model B detects 59 cases (0 net gain, 132 swaps).

Confirms priority shifts occur across architectures, while overall yield gains remain modest (+0 to +2 cases).

07 Limitations & Scope

  • Surveillance vs Diagnosis: Single venous HbA1c ≥ 6.5% represents an elevated screening criterion, not an autonomous clinical diagnosis.
  • Inference Bounds: Separate survey-weighted confidence intervals overlap; paired variance estimation is required for confirmatory testing.
  • Operational Scope: Assumes 100% screening adherence among prioritized adults and uniform laboratory costs.

08 Conclusion & Next Steps

Augmenting administrative diagnosis labels with objective biomarker ground truth modestly refines borderline triage (+2 cases, 53 → 55) without expanding predictor requirements. Next steps require evaluating formal paired complex survey variance and conducting prospective validation in diverse clinical care settings.
